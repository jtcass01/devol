\section{Results}\label{sec:Results}
The performance of the EKF, PF, and DR algorithms was evaluated using an automated Robot Operating System (ROS 2) test harness. 
To ensure statistical significance, each algorithmic configuration underwent 10 independent trials across varying random seeds. 
All quantitative results are reported as the mean alongside their 95\% Confidence Interval (CI).
The algorithms were tested under two primary conditions: a baseline navigation scenario and a ``kidnapped robot'' scenario designed to test global localization recovery. 
To fully evaluate the PF, it was tested across four distinct configurations: PF\_100, PF\_500, PF\_2000, and PF\_5000.

\subsection{Kidnap Recovery}
Robustness to catastrophic tracking failure was evaluated using the kidnapping methodology described previously. 
For analytical purposes in post-processing, the exact start of the kidnapping event was registered when the position error instantaneously exceeded 1.0 meters. 
Recovery time was calculated as the duration required for the algorithm to reduce the tracking error back below 0.3 meters. 

The tests confirmed that the PF variants were the only evaluated algorithms capable of global recovery. 
Following the simulated teleportation, the PF successfully reconverged on the true state, with recovery times improving at higher particle counts.

Because the EKF and DR algorithms rely entirely on continuous local tracking, they were unable to resolve the sudden global displacement. 
Both the EKF and DR completely failed to recover and maintained an error above the threshold for the remainder of the simulation duration.
